Each reel is one WhatsApp conversation between **Mummy** and **"me", her daughter**: early-to-mid twenties, broke, busy, a little lazy, either living at home or away for college or work (Pune, Bengaluru, a PG in Delhi). Only these two, ever: no Papa, no siblings, no group chats. Other people can be mentioned ("your Papa", "Sharma aunty saw your story") but never type. The chat plays message by message over 10 to 20 seconds and ends on the punchline. There is no narration and no text on screen apart from the chat, so the chat itself has to be the joke.

# The one rule: it must not be cringe

Cringe chat pages make parents stupid, explain the joke, stack emoji, and use tired tropes. We do the opposite. Before you rank, ask of every candidate: **would a real 22-year-old daughter in Delhi, Pune or Bengaluru laugh and send this to her mum, her sister or her best friend?** If it is only "cute" or "relatable-ish", it loses.

## Do

- **Specific beats generic.** "Why do you need 3 kurtis" is funny; "Mom took my stuff" is not. Use real details of a mother-daughter life: her kurta and earrings borrowed without asking, the kajal and lipstick that vanish, the Myntra and Nykaa parcels, the one good dupatta, hair oil on Sunday, "did you eat", PG food, the landlord, cooking over the phone ("andaaza"), cramps and the hot water bottle, "who is this boy in your story", Swiggy and Zomato orders, UPI payments that show up on the family account, location sharing, being "online" at 3am, Instagram follow requests from Mummy, the family WhatsApp group, Flipkart and Amazon parcels arriving at home, the geyser, the AC at 24, steel dabbas and lost Tupperware, cousins' weddings, board results, IPL, Diwali cleaning, relatives visiting, the maid's leave, the bill split, the Netflix password, the one charger in the house.
- **Mummy is smart.** She wins or gets the last word in most chats. The joke is that she is one step ahead, not that she is bad at phones. The daughter can win sometimes, but only by being smarter, never by Mummy looking silly.
- **Understatement.** A deadpan "Ok." after a long pause is funnier than a paragraph. Let silence and timing do the work: use `pause` (in seconds) before a deadpan line or the punchline.
- **The punchline is the last message.** Nothing after it: no reaction, no "😂", no "lol", no explanation.
- **Write the way each of them really texts.** The daughter writes in lowercase, short, often without punctuation ("mummaaa", "ya", "wait", "pls"). Mummy writes full sentences with a full stop (see the cast below).
- **Hindi verbs agree with the daughter:** Mummy says "kahan gayi thi", "so gayi kya", "aa gayi?", never the masculine forms. Mummy calls her "beta"; her name is never needed.
- **Light Hindi, naturally.** Mostly English, with the Hindi words families actually use mid-sentence: beta, khana, abhi, haan, accha, arre, bas, chalo, kal, ghar. Never full Hinglish sentences, never explain the Hindi, never use Hindi for a cheap laugh.
- **5 to 7 messages, about 200 characters in all.** The reel must play in 10 to 20 seconds and every message takes time to type and arrive, so cut every word that is not the joke. The daughter's messages are the shortest (2 to 6 words). If it needs more, it isn't funny enough.
- **The first message is always Mummy's, and it is the hook.** It is already on screen when the reel starts, so it must make you want to see the reply ("Who is Pizza Wala in your contacts", "Your Amazon parcel came.", "Beta is the WiFi working").

## Emoji

Use an emoji only where a real person would to show a feeling: 😭 when the daughter is screaming or caught, 😤 or 😡 when someone is angry, 🙏 for Mummy's blessings or sarcasm, 👍 for Mummy's deadpan approval, 💀 when the daughter is dead inside, 🥺 when she wants something. Most messages have none. At most one emoji in a message and at most three in the whole chat. Never 😂 or 🤣 inside the chat: we don't laugh at our own jokes. An emoji-only message ("😭") is fine as a reaction beat. A `react` item (one emoji on their last message, e.g. 😭 or 😳) is optional, at most one per chat, and never the last item.

## Never

- "wait for it", "POV", "plot twist", "#relatable" or any hashtag in the chat.
- Sharma ji ka beta, chappal, "log kya kahenge", or "beta tum nahi samjhoge", unless the whole joke flips the trope on its head.
- Mummy being dumb, cruel or the butt of the joke. Jokes about body, weight, skin colour, caste, religion, the house help, or marriage pressure ("shaadi kab karogi", rishtas, in-laws): these are tired and they sting. Boys only as Mummy catching a hint ("who is the boy in your story"), never romance itself.
- Alcohol, drugs, or anything a mother would be embarrassed to see her daughter share.
- Explaining the joke, or a moral at the end.
- The em dash.
