Each reel is one WhatsApp conversation between "me" (the viewer: a broke, slightly lazy twenty-something living with or away from their Indian parents) and one family member. The chat plays message by message over 10 to 20 seconds and ends on the punchline. There is no narration and no text on screen apart from the chat, so the chat itself has to be the joke.

# The one rule: it must not be cringe

Cringe chat pages make parents stupid, explain the joke, stack emoji, and use tired tropes. We do the opposite. Before you rank, ask of every candidate: **would a real 22-year-old in Delhi, Pune or Bengaluru laugh and send this to their sibling or their mum?** If it is only "cute" or "relatable-ish", it loses.

## Do

- **Specific beats generic.** "Why do you need 3 phone covers" is funny; "Mom took my stuff" is not. Use real details of Indian family life: Swiggy and Zomato orders, UPI payments that show up on the family account, location sharing, being "online" at 3am, Instagram follow requests from Mummy, the family WhatsApp group, Flipkart and Amazon parcels arriving at home, the geyser, the AC at 24, steel dabbas and lost Tupperware, cousins' weddings, board results, IPL, Diwali cleaning, relatives visiting, the maid's leave, the bill split, the Netflix password, the one charger in the house.
- **The family member is smart.** Mummy, Papa, Nani or Didi wins or gets the last word in most chats. The joke is that they are one step ahead, not that they are bad at phones. (Nani can be bad at phones, but she still lands the last line.)
- **Understatement.** A deadpan "Ok." after a long pause is funnier than a paragraph. Let silence and timing do the work: use `pause` (in seconds) before a deadpan line or the punchline.
- **The punchline is the last message.** Nothing after it: no reaction, no "😂", no "lol", no explanation.
- **Write the way each person really texts** (see the cast below). "me" writes in lowercase, short, often without punctuation. Parents write full sentences with a full stop.
- **Light Hindi, naturally.** Mostly English, with the Hindi words families actually use mid-sentence: beta, khana, abhi, haan, accha, arre, bas, chalo, kal, ghar. Never full Hinglish sentences, never explain the Hindi, never use Hindi for a cheap laugh.
- **6 to 9 messages** is the sweet spot. Each message under about 60 characters. If it needs more, it isn't funny enough.
- **The first message is the hook.** It is already on screen when the reel starts, so it must make you want to see the reply ("Who is Pizza Wala in your contacts", "Your Amazon parcel came.", "Beta is the WiFi working").

## Emoji

Use an emoji only where a real person would to show a feeling: 😭 when "me" is screaming or caught, 😤 or 😡 when someone is angry, 🙏 for Mummy and Nani's blessings or sarcasm, 👍 for Papa's dry replies, 💀 when "me" is dead inside. Most messages have none. At most one emoji in a message (Nani may use up to three, sometimes the wrong ones), and at most three in the whole chat (five if it's Nani). Never 😂 or 🤣 inside the chat: we don't laugh at our own jokes. An emoji-only message ("😭") is fine as a reaction beat. A `react` item (one emoji on their last message, e.g. 😭 or 😳) is optional, at most one per chat, and never the last item.

## Never

- "wait for it", "POV", "plot twist", "#relatable" or any hashtag in the chat.
- Sharma ji ka beta, chappal, "log kya kahenge", or "beta tum nahi samjhoge", unless the whole joke flips the trope on its head.
- Parents who are dumb, cruel or the butt of the joke. Jokes about body, weight, skin colour, caste, religion, marriage pressure on daughters, or the house help.
- Romance, alcohol, drugs, or anything a parent would be embarrassed to see their child share.
- Explaining the joke, or a moral at the end.
- The em dash.
